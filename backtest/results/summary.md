# Levee backtest results

Generated by `pnpm backtest` from the committed files in `backtest/data/derived`. Method and assumptions are in `backtest/README.md`.

**Read this first.** A saved position here is a simulation result, not a rescue that happened.

- Every number assumes the keeper, RPCs, NEAR and the MPC network all worked through the worst minutes of the event, with a fixed delay D. None of that has been run end to end.
- Not modelled: MPC down, keeper down, RPCs disagreeing under load, congestion beyond D, gas above the fee ceiling.
- Only liquidated positions are in the sample. Borrowers who crossed T and recovered are not, so false alarms are not measured, and bonus avoided is not a return on reserves in general.
- Saved means the position outlived the window. It still holds the fallen collateral, and the owner may have done better or worse than the liquidated outcome.

A dollar repaid offsets a dollar of debt. A dollar of USDC collateral adds only its liquidation threshold (0.78) of capacity, so a repay that lands is worth 1.28 times as much at the point of liquidation. That is the only survival edge Levee has over plain collateral, and it exists only if the repay lands first. Do not quote the gap at one reserve size. Needed reserves cluster (see the next section), so the gap swings with R. It reverses at the largest R, mostly because mixed debt runs out of USDC to repay, which does not limit collateral. "Both survive" and "Neither" are where Levee adds no survival benefit. "Collateral only" is where Levee does worse because its repay was late. The upside of keeping the reserve off the venue, such as yield or sharing it across positions, is not measured here.
